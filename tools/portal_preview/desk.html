Verify tile contents

Optionally decode the eligible tiles on this device. Runs up to 128 tiles per batch, with a time limit; continue until the unchecked count reaches zero. Large or complex packs may need several batches.

Pause keeps completed results after the current operation finishes. Close / cancel clears the check immediately. Unreadable can mean unsupported data or a limit in this browser. Decoding does not verify map accuracy, complete rendering or safe passage.

The initial check reads presence and blob size. Optional content verification adds a decoding result to each eligible tile; reports explicitly include unchecked tiles. Includes both sides of tile boundaries and corners, with no line across segment breaks. Straight GPX edges use the shorter span across the date line. Supported packs and limits

Use a trusted, fully exported single .mbtiles file of at most 64 MiB. This browser viewer requires ordinary metadata/tiles tables and a unique tile-coordinate index. It accepts 256 × 256 or 512 × 512 still PNG, JPEG and WebP tiles, zooms 0–22, and MBTiles/TMS rows. A tile may be at most 2 MiB; a displayed frame is limited to 16 MiB of compressed tiles. Vector packs declared as PBF may contain raw or gzip Mapbox Vector Tiles (versions 1 or 2). Gzip needs browser DecompressionStream support. Each expanded tile is limited to 8 MiB, 64 layers, 5,000 features and 50,000 positions; a frame is limited to 10,000 features and 100,000 positions. Geometry must fit the supported tile buffer (−1 to 2 tile widths). Point names are limited to 48 characters, 32 per tile and 96 per frame, with overlapping names omitted. Compatible larger packs can use the desktop map viewer. Normalized or view-based databases and WAL-mode files need a compatible flat, indexed, rollback-mode export first.

Your file is read into memory and is not modified. File and database reads stop after 15 seconds; Close / cancel also stops the reader. A new file or frame clears previous selection. Keep map attribution and your original pack. This viewer does not audit every tile, acquire maps, follow your GPS, calculate routes or verify current conditions.

Supported files and limits

WGS 84 GeoJSON (RFC 7946), with longitude first and latitude second in the file. Supports Point, MultiPoint, LineString, MultiLineString, Polygon with holes, MultiPolygon and GeometryCollection. Up to 1,000 features, 10,000 positions and 2,000 geometry parts per file. Blank geometries are counted; malformed or unsupported geometry rejects the whole file. Polygon rings must close. Legacy CRS declarations and edges spanning more than 180° longitude are rejected; export date-line crossings as separate parts.

Property text is displayed as text, never as links or code. Feature exports preserve geometry, properties and optional ID; other top-level feature metadata is omitted. No topology, source accuracy or access-rights check is performed. Keep the original file. MBTiles, PBF/vector tiles, shapefiles, KML and GeoTIFF do not open in this viewer.

Collection JSON saves every place; CSV and GPX use your search results.

Reopen collection JSON here to retain the original numbers and labels. CSV works with the FieldForge GPS place catalog. GPX works with the desktop Places import. Both export coordinates to seven decimal places; that does not establish their accuracy. Exports contain names, precise locations and source notes in plain text.

Import formats and what is kept

Collection JSON must be a FieldForge place collection. CSV needs name,latitude,longitude and optionally source; other columns are rejected. Use UTF-8 and WGS 84 decimal degrees. Imports add records and skip identical name/coordinate/source combinations. An invalid record rejects the whole file.

This collection keeps names, coordinates and source notes. It does not keep GPX tracks, elevation or additional metadata. Keep your original files. Clearing a GPX or image does not remove places already copied into this collection. CSV adds a protective apostrophe to formula-like labels; JSON preserves your original text. GPX writes +180° longitude as the equivalent −180° meridian.
